## Fill Types Explained: What Actually Suits Your Sleep Style

This is where most buyers go wrong. They pick a pillow based on how it looks in a photo or how it feels for 10 seconds in a store, not based on fill material and what that means for their sleep position.

**Memory Foam (Solid or Shredded)**
Solid memory foam offers firm, consistent support. It works well for side sleepers with broad shoulders who need a high loft. The downside is heat retention and the fact that you cannot adjust the height. Shredded memory foam, like what is inside the **Coop Home Goods Eden**, lets you remove fill to lower the loft.

**Latex**
Latex is more responsive than memory foam, meaning it pushes back rather than sinking in. It sleeps cooler and holds its shape longer. The **Purple Harmony Pillow** uses a solid latex core that many back and combination sleepers find ideal. Latex pillows tend to be heavier and more expensive, running $150 to $250 CAD.

**Down and Down Alternative**
Traditional down is incredibly soft and mouldable, but it collapses quickly and needs regular fluffing. Down alternative (synthetic fill) is hypoallergenic and cheaper. Neither works well for side sleepers who need consistent neck support, but stomach sleepers often do best with a soft, flat down or down-alternative pillow.

**Buckwheat**
Buckwheat hulls are adjustable, firm, and sleep cool. They are also loud when you move. A common complaint among new buckwheat pillow users is the crinkling noise, which becomes less noticeable over a few weeks but is a real trade-off.

## Pillow Loft: The Detail Most Buyers Ignore

Loft is the height of the pillow when it is lying flat. Getting this wrong is the most common reason people buy a pillow and still wake up with neck pain.

Side sleepers generally need a high loft (4 to 6 inches) to fill the gap between their shoulder and head. Back sleepers do best with a medium loft (3 to 5 inches). Stomach sleepers need a low loft (2 to 3 inches) or no pillow at all, since too much height cranks the neck upward and puts stress on the cervical spine.

The Harvard Health Publishing blog has noted that sleeping position plays a significant role in spinal alignment, and that pillow support should match how you sleep, not just how the pillow feels to the touch. Adjustable pillows like the **Coop Eden** or **Endy Pillow** are genuinely useful because your ideal loft changes based on your mattress firmness too. A softer mattress lets your shoulders sink in, which effectively lowers the gap your pillow needs to fill.

## How Long Should a Pillow Last? And When to Replace Yours

A general guideline from sleep health sources is to replace most pillows every 1 to 2 years, though quality latex pillows can last 3 to 5 years with proper care.

A quick test: fold your pillow in half and let go. If it does not spring back, it has lost its support. Memory foam pillows do not spring back the same way, so for those, check whether the foam has developed permanent body impressions or smells musty.

Pillowcase hygiene matters too. The Sleep Foundation recommends washing pillow covers weekly and washing the pillow itself every 3 to 6 months. Most synthetic and down-alternative pillows are machine washable. Latex and solid memory foam are spot-clean only.
