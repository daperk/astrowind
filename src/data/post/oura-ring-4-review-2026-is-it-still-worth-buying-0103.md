## Quick Verdict / TL;DR

The **Oura Ring 4** remains one of the most accurate, comfortable sleep trackers on the market in 2026. It excels at sleep stage tracking, heart rate variability (HRV) monitoring, and long battery life without the bulk of a smartwatch. The mandatory subscription (around $5.99 per month) is a real ongoing cost to factor in. It is not the best choice for people who want GPS, real-time workout feedback, or detailed step-by-step coaching. For sleep-obsessed users willing to pay the premium, it is hard to beat.

## What the Oura Ring 4 Actually Tracks

The **Oura Ring 4** uses a combination of infrared LED sensors, a red LED sensor, and an accelerometer to collect data while you sleep and throughout the day.

Key metrics it tracks:

- Sleep stages (light, deep, REM)
- Total sleep time and sleep efficiency
- Resting heart rate
- Heart rate variability (HRV)
- Blood oxygen saturation (SpO2)
- Skin temperature variation
- Respiratory rate
- Daytime activity and steps
- Stress and readiness scores

The ring produces three daily scores: Sleep Score, Activity Score, and Readiness Score. The Readiness Score pulls data from multiple signals and gives you a single number meant to reflect how recovered your body is. Many users find this genuinely useful for deciding whether to push hard in a workout or take it easy.

One thing worth clarifying: the ring does not give you a raw data dump. It interprets data for you. If you want full access to every raw number, you will need to explore the app settings or export options, which are available but not front-and-center.

## Sleep Tracking Accuracy: How Good Is It Really?

This is the question most people care about. Independent comparisons (including research reviewed by organizations like the Sleep Foundation) consistently show consumer sleep trackers have limitations in matching polysomnography (the clinical gold standard). That said, the **Oura Ring 4** generally performs better than wrist-based wearables for sleep stage detection, largely because finger-based optical sensors get a cleaner blood flow signal.

In daily use, the ring is reliable at:

- Detecting when you fell asleep and woke up
- Identifying REM sleep with reasonable consistency
- Tracking HRV trends over time (trends matter more than single-night readings)

Where it is less precise:

- Deep sleep amounts are frequently estimated, not measured directly
- It can misread light sleep if you lie still and awake in bed
- SpO2 readings are spot-checked, not continuous through the night

For practical sleep improvement, the trend data over weeks matters far more than any single night. The **Oura Ring 4** handles long-term trend tracking well, which is where it earns its value.

## Battery Life, Comfort, and Wearability

This is where the **Oura Ring 4** genuinely shines compared to smartwatches. Most users get 7 to 8 days of battery life per charge in real-world use, sometimes closer to 6 days if you are doing a lot of daytime heart rate tracking.

The ring itself is titanium with a smooth interior coating designed to reduce skin irritation. It comes in multiple finishes (silver, black, gold, stealth, and brushed titanium) at similar price points.

Sizing requires ordering a free sizing kit first, which Oura ships to you. Getting the right size matters a lot for accuracy. The ring should sit snugly but not cut off circulation.

Comfort considerations:

- Most people forget they are wearing it after a day or two
- The charging cradle is small and easy to pack when traveling
- Some users with larger knuckles find putting it on and off uncomfortable
- It is not a good fit for trades or jobs where rings are a safety hazard

The ring is waterproof (rated to 100 meters), so swimming and showering are fine.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is the Oura Ring 4 accurate for sleep tracking?**
It is among the more accurate consumer sleep trackers available, particularly for HRV and sleep timing. Sleep stage detection (light, deep, REM) is estimated, not clinically measured, and will not match a medical sleep study. For tracking trends over time, it performs well.

**Q: Do you have to pay the monthly subscription to use the Oura Ring 4?**
Yes. Without the subscription (approximately $5.99 per month), access to sleep scores, readiness scores, and historical trend data is limited. Basic health metrics are visible, but the core value of the ring is behind the paywall.

**Q: How long does the Oura Ring 4 battery last?**
Most users report 6 to 8 days of battery life depending on usage. Frequent daytime heart rate tracking or heavy SpO2 monitoring will drain it faster. The charging time is roughly 20 to 80 minutes from empty to full.

**Q: Can the Oura Ring 4 detect sleep apnea?**
No. The **Oura Ring 4** tracks blood oxygen saturation and respiratory rate, which can flag irregular patterns, but it is not a medical device and cannot diagnose sleep apnea. If you suspect sleep apnea, talk to your doctor and pursue a proper sleep study. This post is not medical advice.

**Q: How does the Oura Ring 4 compare to the Samsung Galaxy Ring?**
The **Oura Ring 4** generally provides deeper sleep tracking data and a more developed sleep-specific app. The **Samsung Galaxy Ring** skips the subscription fee and integrates tightly with Samsung Health. Your choice depends on whether you are in the Samsung ecosystem and how much the ongoing cost matters to you.

The **Oura Ring 4** earns its reputation as the best ring-based sleep tracker available in 2026.
